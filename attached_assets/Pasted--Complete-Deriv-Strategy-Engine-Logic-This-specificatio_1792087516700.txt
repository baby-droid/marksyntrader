# Complete Deriv Strategy Engine Logic

This specification combines Over 1–Over 7, Under 8–Under 1, automatic run selection from 1–20, Matches/Differs, Rise/Fall, and Even/Odd into one market-analysis and execution framework.

The key requirement is that the LLM must build a strategy engine that can analyze each contract independently, select a suitable market, identify entry conditions, calculate risk, and decide when not to trade. It must not force a trade simply because a strategy has been selected.

## 1. Universal market-analysis engine

Every strategy should use the same data pipeline but apply its own contract-specific rules.

1000 ticks — Historical baseline

Digit distribution, historical frequency, volatility, parity, and regime

100 ticks — Local context

Recent changes, distribution shifts, clustering, and trend context

50 ticks — Confirmation

Confirm whether the candidate strategy still fits recent data

20 ticks — Flow

Detect current clustering, directional movement, and conflicting signals

10 ticks — Entry timing

Check the latest setup, but do not treat a short sequence as proof of the next outcome

Use a rolling buffer for each market. Every new tick updates the shorter windows and the 1000-tick history. Do not mix ticks from different markets or use future ticks when backtesting a past signal.

Important statistical limitation: digit frequencies can help describe past behavior, but a low-frequency digit is not necessarily due to appear next. The same applies to Odd/Even and Matches/Differs. Entry conditions must be tested against subsequent outcomes rather than assumed to predict them.

## 2. Over and Under contract logic

For the standard last-digit contracts, define the predicted final digit as \\(d\\), where \\(d\in\\{0,\ldots,9\\}\\).

- Over \\(b\\) wins when \\(d>b\\).
- Under \\(b\\) wins when \\(d\<b\\).
- The barrier digit itself loses for both contracts.

That last rule is essential: Over 3 requires a digit of 4–9; Under 3 requires a digit of 0–2.

| Contract | Winning digits | Losing digits |
| -------- | -------------- | ------------- |
| Over 1   | 2–9            | 0–1           |
| Over 2   | 3–9            | 0–2           |
| Over 3   | 4–9            | 0–3           |
| Over 4   | 5–9            | 0–4           |
| Over 5   | 6–9            | 0–5           |
| Over 6   | 7–9            | 0–6           |
| Over 7   | 8–9            | 0–7           |
| Under 8  | 0–7            | 8–9           |
| Under 7  | 0–6            | 7–9           |
| Under 6  | 0–5            | 6–9           |
| Under 5  | 0–4            | 5–9           |
| Under 4  | 0–3            | 4–9           |
| Under 3  | 0–2            | 3–9           |
| Under 2  | 0–1            | 2–9           |
| Under 1  | 0              | 1–9           |

The implementation must confirm the exact contract type and barrier supported by the selected Deriv market before requesting a proposal.

### 2.1 Over/Under probability engine

For every barrier, calculate the historical probability of a winning digit in each window.

For Over 3:

\\[ P(\text{win})=\sum\_{d=4}^{9}P(d) \\]

For Under 3:

\\[ P(\text{win})=\sum\_{d=0}^{2}P(d) \\]

Calculate these values for the 1000-, 100-, 50-, 20-, and 10-tick windows.

Example:

```
Contract: OVER 3

1000T estimated win frequency: 62%
100T estimated win frequency: 67%
50T estimated win frequency: 70%
20T estimated win frequency: 65%
10T estimated win frequency: 60%

Assessment:
Long-term baseline: favorable historically
Recent confirmation: favorable
Immediate flow: mixed
Entry: WAIT
```

These percentages are hypothetical observed frequencies, not guaranteed future probabilities. In particular, a 10-tick sample is very small: 6 winning observations out of 10 does not establish a reliable 60% future win probability.

### 2.2 Contract-specific candidate selection

For each market, evaluate every permitted Over/Under barrier independently.

For each candidate, calculate:

- `baseline_probability`: winning-digit frequency in 1000 ticks.
- `local_probability`: winning-digit frequency in 100 ticks.
- `confirmation_probability`: winning-digit frequency in 50 ticks.
- `flow_probability`: winning-digit frequency in 20 ticks.
- `entry_probability`: winning-digit frequency in 10 ticks.
- `sample_uncertainty`: uncertainty due to the number of observations.
- `distribution_shift`: whether recent digit distribution differs from baseline.
- `quoted_payout`: payout from the actual Deriv proposal.
- `expected_value`: expected net profit based on calibrated probability and quoted payout.
- `signal_validity`: whether the market data and proposal are current.

Do not select Over 7 simply because its losing digits are frequent, or Under 1 simply because zero is underrepresented. Compare the actual probability of winning and the actual offered payout.

### 2.3 Example of barrier selection

Suppose a market produces these illustrative estimates:

| Contract | Estimated win probability | Net profit if successful per 1 unit staked |
| -------- | ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| Over 1   | 88%                       | 0.10                                       |
| Over 3   | 65%                       | 0.65                                       |
| Over 5   | 43%                       | 1.30                                       |
| Over 7   | 19%                       | 3.50                                       |

These figures are illustrative only. A high probability does not necessarily mean a better trade.

For a contract risking 1 unit and earning net profit \\(r\\) on a win:

\\[ EV=p r-(1-p) \\]

For Over 3 in the example:

\\[ EV=(0.65)(0.65)-(0.35)=0.0725 \\]

The estimated EV is +0.0725 units per stake before any additional costs or model error. The engine must use the actual quoted payout and a calibrated probability estimate, not these sample numbers.

Entry rule: choose a contract only if the estimated edge remains positive after accounting for uncertainty, payout, and a safety margin. Otherwise return `NO TRADE`.

## 3. Over/Under entry pattern logic

The engine should distinguish between digit distribution and entry timing. The fact that several losing-range digits appeared recently is not evidence that a winning-range digit is now due.

For each barrier, divide the digit set into:

- `winning_range`
- `losing_range`
- `barrier_digit`
- `winning_frequency`
- `losing_frequency`
- `recent_winning_range_count`
- `recent_losing_range_count`
- `distribution_shift`
- `current_signal_quality`

Example for Over 3:

```
Winning range: 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9
Losing range:  0, 1, 2, 3

1000T: baseline distribution
100T: local distribution
50T: confirmation
20T: flow
10T: entry timing
```

The engine may flag a candidate when the winning-range frequency is favorable in the historical baseline and remains favorable in the recent data. It should not automatically buy after 2–5 losing-range observations, because that sequence alone does not make a winning digit more likely.

### Over/Under decision states

```
NO DATA
   ↓
BASELINE READY
   ↓
CANDIDATE FOUND
   ↓
50T CONFIRMATION
   ↓
20T FLOW CHECK
   ↓
10T ENTRY CHECK
   ↓
PAYOUT / EXPECTED-VALUE CHECK
   ↓
RISK CHECK
   ↓
SIGNAL READY or NO TRADE
```

If the windows conflict, the payout is poor, or the signal is stale, the engine should wait and rescan.

# 4. Automatic run selection: 1–20 runs

The run selector must define what a run means. For this specification, treat a run as one executed contract attempt, not as a guaranteed number of consecutive wins. If the user instead means a contract duration of 1–20 ticks, that needs a separate duration setting.

The goal is to determine how many attempts the bot is permitted to make during a strategy cycle, based on signal quality, risk limits, and the remaining session budget.

## 4.1 Inputs to the run selector

```
account_balance
available_session_risk
stake_per_trade
daily_loss_limit_remaining
session_profit_target_remaining
consecutive_losses
consecutive_wins
market_quality_score
strategy_score
validated_edge
recent_out_of_sample_performance
signal_expiry
```

The selector should also know the actual contract payout and the maximum possible loss per attempt.

## 4.2 Run eligibility

First determine whether a trade is eligible. Only then select the maximum number of attempts.

Example decision table:

| Conditions                                                               | Permitted run plan                                 |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------- |
| Invalid data, weak edge, conflicting windows                             | 0 runs; wait                                       |
| Candidate but not fully validated                                        | 0 executed runs; continue testing                  |
| One strong, independently validated setup                                | 1 run                                              |
| Stable conditions and sufficient risk budget                             | Up to 2–3 attempts, each independently revalidated |
| Proven strategy with sufficient out-of-sample evidence and risk capacity | Consider a higher cap, up to the configured 20     |
| Daily loss limit reached, stale signal, or circuit breaker triggered     | 0 additional runs                                  |

These are maximum permitted attempts, not a target the bot must complete. The default should be conservative; reaching 20 must never be a requirement.

## 4.3 Adaptive run score

Calculate a normalized quality score:

\\[ Q=0.30S+0.25E+0.20M+0.15D+0.10R \\]

Where:

- \\(S\\) = signal quality, normalized from 0–100.
- \\(E\\) = estimated edge quality, normalized from 0–100.
- \\(M\\) = multi-window agreement, normalized from 0–100.
- \\(D\\) = data quality, normalized from 0–100.
- \\(R\\) = risk capacity, normalized from 0–100.

These weights are initial engineering defaults and must be validated. They do not mean a score of 90 corresponds to a 90% chance of winning.

Use the score to propose a run cap, then apply all hard risk constraints:

```
IF data_quality < minimum:
    max_runs = 0

ELSE IF expected_value <= safety_margin:
    max_runs = 0

ELSE IF daily_loss_limit_reached:
    max_runs = 0

ELSE IF consecutive_losses >= configured_stop:
    max_runs = 0

ELSE:
    max_runs = score_based_run_cap
    max_runs = MIN(max_runs, configured_cap)
    max_runs = MIN(max_runs, affordable_risk_cap)
```

The maximum cap is configurable from 1 to 20. The system can return fewer attempts, including zero, when the evidence or risk budget is insufficient.

## 5. Matches and Differs strategy

These are digit contracts and should use the same 1000/100/50/20/10-tick framework, but their probability calculations differ from Over/Under.

### 5.1 Matches — `DIGITMATCH`

The strategy selects a specific digit from 0–9 as the barrier.

For each digit \\(d\\), calculate:

\\[ P(d)=\frac{\text{number of occurrences of }d}{\text{ticks observed}} \\]

Analyze:

- Digit frequency in 1000 ticks.
- Frequency in 100 ticks.
- Frequency in 50 ticks.
- Frequency in 20 ticks.
- Latest 10-tick pattern.
- Time since the digit last appeared.
- Repeated clusters and distribution shifts.
- The quoted payout and estimated expected value.

Example:

```
Candidate digit: 7

1000T frequency: 12.0%
100T frequency:  15.0%
50T frequency:   16.0%
20T frequency:   20.0%
10T frequency:   10.0%

Interpretation:
Historical concentration: elevated
Recent confirmation: elevated
Immediate sample: weak
Decision: WAIT FOR VALIDATION
```

The next digit is not necessarily more likely to be 7 just because 7 has been frequent. The short windows are noisy, so test whether this type of frequency pattern predicts future Matches outcomes.

### 5.2 Matches entry procedure

```
1. Calculate all ten digit frequencies.
2. Rank digits by baseline frequency.
3. Identify candidate digits.
4. Check recent frequency against baseline.
5. Estimate probability with uncertainty adjustment.
6. Check the actual Matches payout.
7. Require positive expected value after a safety margin.
8. Apply risk limits.
9. Execute only if the signal remains valid.
```

Select the digit with the best validated expected value, not necessarily the most frequent digit.

### 5.3 Differs — `DIGITDIFF`

Differs wins when the final digit does not equal the selected barrier digit.

For barrier \\(d\\):

\\[ P(\text{Differs wins})=1-P(d) \\]

If the digit 7 has an estimated probability of 12%, the estimated Differs win frequency against 7 is 88%. This is not automatically profitable: Differs generally has a different payout, and its expected value must be evaluated separately.

The engine should:

1. Estimate each candidate digit's probability.
2. Calculate the probability of differing from that digit.
3. Compare available payouts.
4. Select the candidate with the best sufficiently reliable expected value.
5. Reject the trade if no candidate passes validation.

Do not switch automatically from Matches to Differs after a loss. Recalculate the evidence and compare both strategies again.

## 6. Rise and Fall strategy

Rise/Fall is based on price movement, not last-digit parity.

### Rise — `CALL`

Evaluate:

- Positive price displacement.
- Upward movement ratio.
- Momentum and acceleration.
- Higher highs and higher lows.
- Agreement across 1000/100/50/20 ticks.
- Immediate entry condition from the latest data.
- Contract duration and offered payout.

### Fall — `PUT`

Apply the inverse criteria:

- Negative price displacement.
- Downward movement ratio.
- Negative momentum.
- Lower highs and lower lows.
- Multi-window bearish agreement.
- Valid entry and payout.

### Rise/Fall entry gate

```
IF long-term regime is bullish
AND local trend is bullish
AND short-term flow confirms
AND no unresolved reversal exists
AND expected value passes
AND risk limits pass:
    RISE CANDIDATE

ELSE IF long-term regime is bearish
AND local trend is bearish
AND short-term flow confirms
AND no unresolved reversal exists
AND expected value passes
AND risk limits pass:
    FALL CANDIDATE

ELSE:
    NO TRADE
```

A market's label alone must not trigger a contract. A Bull market can temporarily fall, and a Bear market can temporarily rise.

For Rise/Fall contracts, validate the specific contract duration as well. A directional trend can be real but still fail to produce the required movement within a short duration.

## 7. Even and Odd strategy

Use the parity classification:

- Even: `0, 2, 4, 6, 8`
- Odd: `1, 3, 5, 7, 9`

For each window calculate:

```
odd_count
even_count
odd_percentage
even_percentage
parity_runs
alternation_rate
parity_distribution_shift
```

### Odd — `DIGITODD`

Require:

- An adequately supported baseline Odd estimate.
- Recent confirmation in the 100/50-tick windows.
- Consistent evidence in the 20-tick window.
- A valid 10-tick entry state.
- Positive estimated expected value using the actual payout.

### Even — `DIGITEVEN`

Use the same process with Even as the target.

Do not assume that Odd must follow an Even streak or that Even must follow an Odd streak. A sequence such as `O-O-O-O` does not guarantee that the next digit will be Even.

### Parity example

```
1000T: Odd 54%, Even 46%
100T:  Odd 57%, Even 43%
50T:   Odd 60%, Even 40%
20T:   Odd 55%, Even 45%
10T:   Odd 40%, Even 60%

Decision: WAIT
Reason: immediate parity conflicts with the longer windows
```

A short-window conflict should trigger a recheck rather than an automatic reversal.

## 8. Universal strategy-selection engine

All strategy modules should return the same structured result so the scanner can compare them.

| Strategy  | Primary evidence                              | Contract     |
| --------- | --------------------------------------------- | ------------ |
| Over 1–7  | Probability mass above barrier                | `DIGITOVER`  |
| Under 8–1 | Probability mass below barrier                | `DIGITUNDER` |
| Matches   | Probability of a specified digit              | `DIGITMATCH` |
| Differs   | Probability of not matching a specified digit | `DIGITDIFF`  |
| Rise      | Upward price movement                         | `CALL`       |
| Fall      | Downward price movement                       | `PUT`        |
| Odd       | Odd-digit probability                         | `DIGITODD`   |
| Even      | Even-digit probability                        | `DIGITEVEN`  |

For each strategy, return:

```
market
strategy
contract_type
barrier
duration
baseline_estimate
recent_estimate
uncertainty
quoted_payout
expected_value
signal_score
entry_condition
rejection_reasons
maximum_permitted_runs
risk_status
```

Only the execution layer should turn a validated signal into a purchase request.

## 9. Market scanner and strategy ranking

For every supported market:

1. Discover the available market symbols dynamically.
2. Confirm that the market is active and its data is valid.
3. Load 1000 ticks and maintain rolling windows.
4. Evaluate every enabled strategy.
5. Estimate each contract's probability and uncertainty.
6. Obtain the actual contract proposal and payout.
7. Calculate expected value and apply a safety margin.
8. Reject invalid, stale, conflicting, or over-risk candidates.
9. Rank the remaining candidates by validated expected value, evidence quality, and risk suitability.
10. Select the best eligible candidate—or select no trade.

Do not rank candidates by confidence score alone. A high probability with a poor payout can be inferior to a lower probability with a better payout.

## 10. Backtesting and calibration

Before allowing automatic live execution, test each contract family separately.

The test should use chronological, out-of-sample data and simulate the actual entry timing and payout structure. Track:

- Win rate and loss rate.
- Expected value per trade.
- Maximum drawdown.
- Longest losing streak.
- Results by market and strategy.
- Results by confidence band.
- Results by proposed run count.
- Results under changing volatility and distribution regimes.

Calibrate probability estimates on historical training data and evaluate them on data not used to select the strategy. Avoid tuning thresholds on the same period used to report performance.

A strategy that performs well on one market may not work on another. Keep separate performance estimates by market, contract type, barrier, and duration.

## 11. Master pseudocode for the LLM

```
FOR each active market:

    validate_market_data()

    IF fewer than 1000 usable ticks:
        reject_market()
        CONTINUE

    calculate_windows(1000, 100, 50, 20, 10)

    calculate_digit_distribution()
    calculate_parity_statistics()
    calculate_price_momentum()
    calculate_distribution_shifts()
    calculate_pattern_statistics()
    calculate_uncertainty()

    candidates = []

    FOR each enabled strategy:

        IF strategy is OVER or UNDER:
            evaluate_each_supported_barrier()

        IF strategy is MATCHES or DIFFERS:
            evaluate_each_supported_digit()

        IF strategy is RISE or FALL:
            evaluate_direction_and_duration()

        IF strategy is ODD or EVEN:
            evaluate_parity()

        estimate_probability()
        obtain_actual_contract_payout()
        calculate_expected_value()
        apply_uncertainty_margin()

        IF candidate passes all entry conditions:
            candidates.add(candidate)

    rank_candidates_by:
        expected_value
        evidence_quality
        payout
        risk
        market_suitability

    IF no eligible candidate:
        return NO_TRADE

    selected = best_eligible_candidate()

    determine_maximum_runs(
        selected,
        account_balance,
        risk_budget,
        loss_limits,
        validated_performance
    )

    FOR each permitted attempt:

        recheck_market_data()
        recheck_signal()
        recheck_payout()
        recheck_expected_value()
        recheck_risk_limits()

        IF any check fails:
            cancel_remaining_attempts()
            BREAK

        execute_one_contract()
        monitor_contract_result()
        record_trade()

        IF stop condition reached:
            cancel_remaining_attempts()
            BREAK

        recalculate_all_windows()
        revalidate_before_next_attempt()
```

### Final implementation rule

The bot should calculate the best eligible strategy and the maximum permitted number of attempts, not attempt to predict a guaranteed winning streak. A 20-run plan means permission for up to 20 independently validated attempts—not a forecast of 20 consecutive wins. No strategy should automatically increase stakes or continue trading to recover previous losses.
