# MARK SYN TRADER SIGNAL — Advanced Market Analysis Engine

The goal is to build a structured signal engine that analyzes all the Deriv trade types you listed, ranks the best market, selects the contract and barrier or entry digit, identifies the entry point, and issues a signal only when the evidence is strong enough.

Important: No analysis can guarantee accurate wins on every trade. Digit outcomes can be effectively random, and a digit appearing less frequently does not mean it is due to appear. The engine should measure actual predictive performance, reject weak setups, and use a no-trade signal whenever evidence is insufficient.

## 1. Core analysis engine

Use five rolling windows together. Each window has a specific purpose.

| Window      | Role               | What to analyze                                             |
| ----------- | ------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1,000 ticks | Long-term baseline | Digit frequencies, parity, direction, volatility, stability |
| 100 ticks   | Market structure   | Distribution changes, trends, recurring patterns, streaks   |
| 50 ticks    | Momentum           | Recent strengthening or weakening of a candidate            |
| 20 ticks    | Confirmation       | Whether the setup remains supported                         |
| 10 ticks    | Entry timing       | Immediate trigger, reversal, contradiction, noise           |

For every market, calculate:

- Digit frequencies for 0–9 and their percentages.
- Odd/even proportions.
- Frequencies of Rise, Fall, unchanged ticks and direction reversals.
- Over and Under probabilities for each available barrier.
- Match/Differs candidate-digit statistics.
- Streak length, repetition, gaps and transitions between digits.
- Tick volatility, alternation, entropy and distribution stability.
- Agreement between historical windows and the latest observations.
- Actual contract win rate, payout, expected value and drawdown from recorded trades.

Critical distinction: Frequency, streaks and gaps describe past data; they are not automatically predictive. The engine must validate whether each feature predicts future outcomes on held-out data before giving it significant weight.

## 2. Universal signal decision pipeline

STEP 1 — SCAN MARKETS

Read live ticks, verify symbol availability and update rolling windows.

STEP 2 — ANALYZE CONTRACTS

Evaluate every eligible trade type, digit candidate and barrier.

STEP 3 — VALIDATE THE EDGE

Check out-of-sample accuracy, payout, expected value, window agreement and risk.

STEP 4 — CONFIRM ENTRY

Wait for a fresh trigger; cancel if the setup becomes invalid or expires.

ENTRY NOW

Only if every required gate passes.

NO TRADE

Insufficient evidence, poor value or conflicting signals.

The 10-tick window is a timing filter, not a reliable standalone predictor. A 10-tick sample is too small to establish a robust statistical edge by itself.

Each contract type needs its own probability model. Do not use one generic rule to choose every trade.

## 4. Entry digit and entry-point engine

Keep what to predict separate from when to execute.

### Entry digit selection

For every digit \\(d \in \\{0,\ldots,9\\}\\), calculate:

- `P_NEXT(d)`: calibrated probability of the relevant next-contract outcome.
- `EDGE(d)`: expected net return using the current quoted payout.
- `UNCERTAINTY(d)`: uncertainty in the probability estimate.
- `STABILITY(d)`: whether the estimated edge remains valid across tested periods and market conditions.
- `DATA_QUALITY(d)`: sufficient observations and no missing or stale ticks.

Select the candidate with the best validated expected value, provided it passes the uncertainty and risk checks. If no candidate qualifies, return `NO TRADE`.

Do not assign arbitrary bonuses for digit gaps, streaks or recurrence. Include those features only if testing demonstrates that they improve predictions on unseen data.

### Entry point state machine

### 1

NO SETUP

No validated edge.

### 2

WATCH

A candidate qualifies for monitoring.

### 3

SETUP

The contract, digit or barrier is selected.

### 4

CONFIRMING

Recheck live probability, payout, data freshness and invalidation conditions.

### 5

READY

All conditions pass; prepare the order.

### 6

ENTRY NOW

Submit once, only if the signal is still valid.

Invalidate the signal if its data becomes stale, the selected contract changes, the estimated edge falls below the required threshold, the payout deteriorates, a required confirmation fails, or the execution deadline expires.

A new tick is not necessarily a better entry. The entry rule must match the contract's settlement logic and must be tested against the actual timing and execution delay.

## 5. The most important filter: probability versus payout

The engine must not mistake a high win rate for a profitable strategy.

For a simple contract that pays a net profit \\(r\\) per 1 unit staked on a win and loses the stake on a loss:

\\[ EV = p \cdot r - (1-p) \\]

Where \\(p\\) is the estimated probability of winning and \\(r\\) is the net profit on a winning trade, excluding return of stake.

The break-even probability is:

\\[ p\_{\text{break-even}} = \frac{1}{1+r} \\]

For example, if a contract offers a net profit of 0.80 per 1 unit staked, the break-even probability is approximately 55.6%. A 55% win rate would still have negative expected value under those assumptions.

For actual Deriv contracts, use the live proposal's payout and stake, and calculate expected net profit using the contract's actual payoff rules. Include execution failures, slippage where applicable, and other relevant costs.

## 6. Signal scoring and market ranking

Use a 100-point quality score to rank candidates, but keep it separate from the estimated probability of winning.

| Scoring component                  | Weight |
| ---------------------------------- | ------ |
| Validated predictive edge          | 30     |
| Agreement across tested windows    | 20     |
| Entry trigger and timing quality   | 15     |
| Stability across market conditions | 15     |
| Data quality and freshness         | 10     |
| Payout and execution quality       | 10     |
| Total                              | 100    |

These weights are initial engineering choices, not statistically proven optimal weights. Calibrate them using out-of-sample results.

Suggested operating thresholds:

- Below 65: reject.
- 65–74: watchlist or paper-trading candidate.
- 75–84: candidate for further validation.
- 85–100: highest-priority candidate, but only if it also passes the expected-value, uncertainty and risk gates.

A score of 90/100 must never be reported as a 90% chance of winning. Display those as separate metrics.

### Market selection

For each available market, evaluate each supported contract type and eligible barrier. Then rank candidates by:

1. Positive expected value after the current payout.
2. Reliable out-of-sample predictive performance.
3. Sufficient statistical confidence.
4. Acceptable drawdown and execution risk.
5. Fresh, valid entry conditions.

Return the top three qualifying candidates, or `NO TRADE` if none qualify. Never force a signal just because one market has to be selected.

## 7. Validation before live trading

Before relying on the signal engine, test it on historical data and in paper trading.

- Use chronological train, validation and test periods; do not randomly mix future and past ticks.
- Compare every strategy with an appropriate baseline, including the contract's break-even probability.
- Measure win rate, expected net return, sample size, maximum drawdown, longest losing streak and probability calibration.
- Test different markets, periods and volatility conditions.
- Record every signal, including rejected signals, so performance cannot be judged only from successful examples.
- Account for trying many markets, barriers and strategies, which can produce apparently strong results by chance.
- Revalidate after material changes to the model or market conditions.

For digit strategies in particular, historical frequency differences may be noise. If a strategy cannot demonstrate a stable predictive advantage over a suitable baseline, it should remain in observation mode rather than being promoted to live trading.

## 8. Risk and execution rules

- Keep the stake within a fixed, predefined risk budget.
- Enforce maximum stake, session loss, daily loss and drawdown limits on the execution side.
- Do not use martingale to compensate for weak signals or to recover losses automatically.
- Re-evaluate each run independently instead of blindly executing 1–10 trades.
- Prevent duplicate orders using a unique signal ID and execution idempotency.
- If live data, contract availability, payout, or connection status cannot be verified, do not execute.
- Keep a complete record of signal time, entry quote, contract details, outcome and net profit/loss.
